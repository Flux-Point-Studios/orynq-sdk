## Operator release window

The standing service holds `~/.local/state/orynq-midnight/runner/execution.lock` for its entire lifetime. A proof runner dispatched while it is active waits for that lock and can expire without spending. The two writers therefore run in separate windows. These are prepared instructions for deci at his terminal after review and a separate mainnet authorization; no service was touched during this work.

1. Before recording a new 24-hour approval, deci runs `systemctl --user disable --now orynq-midnight-standing.service`. Verify `systemctl --user is-enabled orynq-midnight-standing.service` reports `disabled` and `systemctl --user is-active orynq-midnight-standing.service` reports `inactive` (both normally exit nonzero). Let its graceful shutdown save the wallet. The enabled user service has `Linger=yes` and `Restart=on-failure` with a 30-second delay; stopping alone does not keep it out of the release window after a reboot.
2. Confirm no other runner owns the lock with `flock --nonblock ~/.local/state/orynq-midnight/runner/execution.lock true`. A nonzero result blocks dispatch; identify the active owner and wait. Never delete the lock file, substitute another path, strip agent guards or start a parallel wallet. This probe does not reserve the lock; the approved runner must acquire that same lock itself.
3. Following the existing reviewed installation and approval procedure, authorize and dispatch exactly the prepared proof-release intent. Keep the standing service disabled and inactive throughout journal reconciliation, retries and proof verification. The standing policy and its spending reservations remain unchanged.
4. If gas is rejected, retain the exact pending bytes. The runner retries with its existing backoff until the journal proves retirement, then prepares against current state and requests a new confirmation. It permits three distinct confirmations per item and enforces the original single-use approval, expiry and shared DUST cap. An ambiguous send, missing history or uncertain finalized scan cannot authorize replacement. A rejected call may wait until its TTL plus the journal margin and finalized scan; do not shorten those guards.
5. Once the proof runner and child have exited, verify the journal, saved wallet state and independent `verifyPosted` results. On success or a safely reconciled failure, deci runs `systemctl --user enable --now orynq-midnight-standing.service` and verifies it is enabled, active and healthy. If the runner still owns the lock or a child is active, resolve that first.

The SDK's direct `post` API permits three immediate attempts after a definitive `1010 / Custom error: 104` transcript refusal, which includes gas exhaustion. It also retries local gas exhaustion before submission. An authority refusal such as custom code 136, transport error or ambiguous send does not trigger replacement. `OperatorWallet.discard` is a no-op when that wallet already freed the refused transaction's DUST. Durable release execution instead waits for journal retirement and re-confirms replacement bytes. Both paths measure current state and add the same headroom. Headroom is finite, not a guarantee against arbitrary concurrent growth.

### Host rebooted or execution interrupted mid-window

These recovery steps are for deci at his terminal. They do not create authorization or permit an agent to drive the runner.

1. Check that the standing service is still disabled and inactive. If it was re-enabled, repeat `disable --now`, allow the wallet to save, and inspect the shared lock and the proof unit `orynq-midnight-runner@<id>.service`. The dispatch timer can resume an interrupted proof unit after boot while its original approval holds. An active proof unit owns recovery; never start a second wallet or delete its lock.
2. From the reviewed installation, run `runner/orynq-midnight-runner status <id>` and `runner/orynq-midnight-runner check <id>`. Inspect that intent's status, audit and attempt log for confirmed hashes, approval identity, expiry, terminal `no`/`done` events and wallet-save outcome. Print selected fields only. A missing status snapshot is recoverable from the audit; a missing journal or confirmed row is not evidence that nothing was sent.
3. Inspect the existing `~/.local/state/orynq-midnight/runner/proof-release.sqlite` without creating or modifying it. For example, `sqlite3 -readonly ~/.local/state/orynq-midnight/runner/proof-release.sqlite 'SELECT id,key,tx_hash,state,broadcasts,ttl_ms,height,block_hash FROM attempts ORDER BY id;'` shows the deploy and circuit-labelled attempts without dumping transaction bytes. Preserve the database and any WAL/SHM files, the intent, approval, audit, used markers, status and saved wallet; never initialize a replacement journal to get past a refusal. Match confirmed hashes against its rows. Journal `landed` is a recovery hint: independently verify each call with `verifyPosted` using its journalled deployment, statement/deadline and the authenticated mainnet trust root. Record exact hashes, blocks and verdicts to establish what actually landed.
4. If the original approval is still admitted, unused and nonterminal, resume that same intent through the installed dispatcher from deci's terminal (`runner/orynq-midnight-runner dispatch`), or let its timer do so. Keep the same journal and deployment address. Landed items are read back; pending items retain exact bytes. Reconciliation queries those hashes before any resend; past-TTL bytes are never resent. Replacement requires journal retirement from finalized chain evidence after TTL plus its margin, a fresh current-state proof and a new confirmation under the original expiry, three-confirmation ceiling and shared fee reservations. A missing row, uncertain scan or ambiguous broadcast blocks replacement.
5. If approval expired or was consumed/refused, do not edit timestamps, remove used markers, erase audit history or force a new deploy. Preserve the partial release and reconcile its confirmed hashes through the reviewed journal's chain-view checks without broadcasting. A pending row stays pending until landed or explicitly retired; elapsed wall time alone cannot close it. To finish remaining items, obtain a separate approval under the existing procedure and resume the same intent/journal with all historical confirmations and reservations retained. To close out without completing, let the existing approval expire (or retain its terminal refusal), verify every confirmed hash is landed or retired, record which of the three items remain incomplete, and verify the proof unit and all children are gone and the saved wallet is usable. Only then restore the standing service with `enable --now` and check its health. If all three landed despite the interruption, record both consensus-verified call results; never mark completion from process exit alone.
