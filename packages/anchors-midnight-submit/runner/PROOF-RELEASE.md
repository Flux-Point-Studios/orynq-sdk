## Operator release window

The standing service holds `~/.local/state/orynq-midnight/runner/execution.lock` for its entire lifetime. A proof runner dispatched while it is active waits for that lock and can expire without spending. The two writers therefore run in separate windows. These are prepared instructions for deci at his terminal after review and a separate mainnet authorization; no service was touched during this work.

1. Before recording a new 24-hour approval, deci runs `systemctl --user stop orynq-midnight-standing.service` and verifies that it is inactive. Let its graceful shutdown save the wallet.
2. Confirm no other runner owns the lock with `flock --nonblock ~/.local/state/orynq-midnight/runner/execution.lock true`. A nonzero result blocks dispatch; identify the active owner and wait. Never delete the lock file, substitute another path, strip agent guards or start a parallel wallet. This probe does not reserve the lock; the approved runner must acquire that same lock itself.
3. Following the existing reviewed installation and approval procedure, authorize and dispatch exactly the prepared proof-release intent. Keep the standing service stopped throughout journal reconciliation, retries and proof verification. The standing policy and its spending reservations remain unchanged.
4. If gas is rejected, retain the exact pending bytes. The runner retries with its existing backoff until the journal proves retirement, then prepares against current state and requests a new confirmation. It permits three distinct confirmations per item and enforces the original single-use approval, expiry and shared DUST cap. An ambiguous send, missing history or uncertain finalized scan cannot authorize replacement. A rejected call may wait until its TTL plus the journal margin and finalized scan; do not shorten those guards.
5. Once the proof runner and child have exited, verify the journal, saved wallet state and independent `verifyPosted` results. On success or a safely reconciled failure, deci runs `systemctl --user start orynq-midnight-standing.service` and checks its startup/health. If the runner still owns the lock or a child is active, resolve that first.

The SDK's direct `post` API permits three immediate attempts only when its wallet reports definitive gas rejection; durable release execution instead waits for journal retirement and re-confirms replacement bytes. Both paths measure current state and add the same headroom. Headroom is finite, not a guarantee against arbitrary concurrent growth.
